# 05 · Compliance, ethics and risk

The counselling business has a bad reputation in India for good reasons.
Operating cleanly is both the right thing and, in a referral-driven market, the
better commercial strategy. This chapter is the "don't get sued, don't get
shut down, don't deserve to be" list.

---

## 1. The seven claims you must never make

| ❌ Never say | ✅ Say instead |
|---|---|
| "Guaranteed MBBS seat" | "We build a realistic, ordered list of options for your rank" |
| "100% placement guarantee" | "We make you a stronger, better-targeted candidate" |
| "We have contacts in the college" | "We help you use the official counselling process well" |
| "Management quota arrange kara denge" | "We can explain what a private seat really costs and what it commits you to" |
| "Visa guaranteed" | "We improve the quality of your application; the decision is the government's" |
| "Our students got AIR 1" (without consent + context) | Nothing, unless you have written post-result consent and can state the material facts |
| "Sabse best consultancy in India" | Specific, verifiable claims only |

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and the CCPA's 2024 coaching-sector
advertising guidelines make unverifiable success claims actionable, with
penalties in lakhs. This applies to your **Instagram captions and WhatsApp
messages** exactly as much as to the website.

**The website already complies.** Every page footer carries the no-guarantee and
no-affiliation statement, and the FAQ answers the guarantee question head-on.
Do not "improve" the marketing by removing them.

## 2. Working with minors

Most NEET/JEE and school clients are under 18. Non-negotiables:

1. **A parent or legal guardian books, consents and pays.** The website form has
   a guardian-consent checkbox; make it a real gate in your process too.
2. **Written consent before any psychometric assessment**, stating what is
   tested, who sees the results and how long they are kept.
3. **Never counsel a minor alone in a closed room without the parent's
   knowledge.** For online sessions, the parent should be told the session is
   happening and be reachable.
4. **No behavioural tracking or targeted advertising aimed at children** — this
   is an explicit DPDP Act prohibition. It is one reason this site ships with no
   ad pixels and self-hosted fonts.
5. **Safeguarding:** if a student discloses self-harm, abuse or acute distress,
   you escalate to the parent and to a qualified professional. Have the
   Tele-MANAS number (**14416**) and two local professionals' numbers written
   down before you take your first student session, not during one.

## 3. Scope boundaries — say "that's not us" out loud

| Situation | Correct response |
|---|---|
| Clinical depression, anxiety, self-harm | Refer to a licensed professional. Give the helpline. Do not "coach" it. |
| Legal question (admission dispute, fee refund case) | Refer to a lawyer |
| Loan structuring or investment advice | Refer to a bank/SEBI-registered adviser |
| Visa filing | Refer to an authorised immigration professional; do not lodge applications |
| Overseas job placement | Do not touch it without an MEA Recruiting Agent licence |
| Medical advice about a condition affecting studies | Refer to a doctor |

Saying "this is outside what we do, here's who can help" costs one fee and buys
a decade of referrals.

---

## 4. Money hygiene

- Publish fees. Charge exactly what is published.
- Issue an **invoice for every rupee**, including cash.
- Take payments **only** into the business account or official payment link —
  never a personal UPI. Say so on the website (the contact and disclaimer pages
  already do).
- **No success-contingent fees** for jobs or admissions.
- **No commission from any institute, lender or university.** If that ever
  changes, disclose it on the website in plain language — an undisclosed
  commission is exactly the conflict of interest that has ruined this industry's
  reputation.
- Honour the refund policy without argument. One clean refund generates more
  referrals than one fought-over ₹5,000 retains.

## 5. Data protection in practice (DPDP Act, 2023)

| Requirement | How you satisfy it |
|---|---|
| Consent notice | The privacy policy page + the form's consent checkbox |
| Purpose limitation | Use enquiry data only to respond to the enquiry |
| Children's data | Guardian consent checkbox; parent books and pays |
| Data minimisation | The form asks only what a counsellor actually needs |
| Retention limits | Stated in the privacy policy — enforce it with a calendar reminder |
| Erasure on request | A real inbox that acts on requests, within 30 days |
| Grievance officer | Named in the privacy policy — put a real person there |
| Security | 2FA on every business account; encrypted laptop; no client data in personal WhatsApp exports |
| Breach notification | Know in advance whom you'd tell and how |

**Practical minimums:** two-factor authentication on email and the leads
dashboard, a password manager, full-disk encryption on the counselling laptop,
and no student data in personal Google Drive folders shared with "anyone with
the link".

## 6. Testimonials and case studies

- Get **written** consent (a WhatsApp message saying "yes, you can use this"
  is written consent — save it).
- For a result-based testimonial, consent must be taken **after** the result.
- Do not use a student's photograph without consent; for minors, the parent's.
- Anonymise case studies by default ("a student from Kanpur with 512 in NEET").
- Never invent a testimonial. The three currently on the site are explicitly
  labelled as samples — **replace or delete them before launch**.

---

## 7. Insurance and paperwork

- **Professional indemnity insurance:** ~₹5,000–15,000/year for a small cover.
  Worth it from the day you take money for advice.
- **Engagement letter** for every paid client: scope, fee, exclusions, no
  guarantee, confidentiality, refund terms.
- **Session notes** kept factual and professional — assume the client could read
  them, because they may be entitled to.
- **Records** of consent, invoices and testimonial permissions, retained for the
  period stated in your privacy policy.

## 9. A note on ethics that isn't about law

This business sits in front of families at their most anxious and least
informed. The pressure to sell the expensive package, to nod along with the
repeat-year fantasy, or to imply an influence you don't have will be constant,
and it will be profitable in the short term.

The whole site has been written on the opposite bet: that saying "no, this isn't
worth your money" out loud is the thing that makes a consultancy referable. In a
market where every competitor is over-promising, being the one who tells the
truth is not just the decent position — it's the only durable differentiator you
have.
