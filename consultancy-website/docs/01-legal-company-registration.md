# 01 · Legal setup & company registration (India)

> **Disclaimer:** This is a practical, researched guide — not legal or tax advice.
> Thresholds, fees and forms change, and several rules are state-specific.
> **Verify everything with a CA or company secretary before filing.** A one-hour
> consultation costs ₹1,000–3,000 and is the cheapest insurance you will ever buy.

---

## 1. Do you even need a company to start?

**No.** In India you can legally provide consulting services as a **sole
proprietor** from day one. There is no incorporation, no ROC filing, no minimum
capital. You need:

- your own PAN,
- a bank account (a current account, once you start invoicing),
- GST registration **only after** you cross the turnover threshold.

Many consultancies over-engineer this at the start, spend ₹25,000 on a Pvt Ltd
they don't need, then let it lapse. **Start as a proprietorship, upgrade when
there is a reason to.**

---

## 2. Choosing the entity

| | Sole Proprietorship | Partnership Firm | LLP | Private Limited |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Setup cost | ₹0–2,000 | ₹2,000–8,000 | ₹6,000–12,000 | ₹8,000–18,000 |
| Setup time | Same day | 3–7 days | 10–20 days | 7–15 days |
| Separate legal identity | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Personal liability | **Unlimited** | Unlimited (joint) | Limited | Limited |
| Owners | 1 | 2–20 | 2+ | 1 (OPC) or 2+ |
| Annual compliance cost | ₹3,000–8,000 | ₹5,000–10,000 | ₹10,000–18,000 | ₹18,000–35,000 |
| Statutory audit | No (unless tax audit applies) | No | Only above ₹40L turnover / ₹25L capital | **Always** |
| Taxed at | Your slab rate | 30% + cess | 30% + cess | 22–25% + surcharge |
| Can raise investment | No | Hard | Hard | Yes |
| Best when | Solo counsellor, testing the market | 2 partners, low formality | 2+ partners, want liability protection | Hiring a team, taking funding, B2B contracts with schools/corporates |

**Recommendation for this client:** start as a **sole proprietorship**. Move to
an **LLP** when a second partner joins or annual revenue crosses ~₹25–30 lakh,
and to a **Private Limited** only if you plan to hire a real team, sign
institutional contracts, or raise money.

A practical middle path many consultancies miss: a proprietorship with **Udyam
registration** looks fully legitimate on invoices, gets you a current account,
and costs nothing.

## 3. Registrations, step by step

### 3.1 Sole proprietorship — the fast path (1–3 days)

| Step | What | Where | Cost | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PAN** (yours, personal) | Already have it | — | — |
| 2 | **Udyam / MSME registration** | udyamregistration.gov.in | **Free** | 10 min, instant certificate |
| 3 | **Shops & Establishment registration** (if you have a commercial premises or employees) | Your state's labour department portal | ₹100–2,000 | 1–7 days |
| 4 | **Current bank account** | Any bank | ₹0–10,000 min balance | 1–3 days |
| 5 | **GST** (only if required — see §4) | gst.gov.in | Free | 3–7 days |
| 6 | **TAN** (only if you will deduct TDS — e.g. paying rent above the threshold or salaries) | tin-nsdl / Protean | ₹65 | 3–5 days |

> Udyam is Aadhaar- + PAN-based, takes ten minutes, and is genuinely free.
> Anyone charging you ₹1,500 for "MSME certificate" is charging for a form you
> can fill yourself. The same is true of "Udyam renewal" — there is no renewal.

**What banks usually ask for a proprietorship current account** (two proofs of
business existence): Udyam certificate, GST certificate, Shops & Establishment
licence, professional tax registration, or a municipal trade licence. Udyam +
one more is normally enough. Carry PAN, Aadhaar, address proof, photos, and the
business address proof (rent agreement + owner NOC, or electricity bill).

### 3.2 LLP (10–20 days)

1. **DSC** (digital signature) for each designated partner — ₹1,000–2,000 each.
2. **Name reservation** via RUN-LLP on the MCA portal.
3. **FiLLiP** form — incorporation, allots DPIN to partners; PAN and TAN are
   issued along with it.
4. **LLP Agreement** — must be filed in **Form 3 within 30 days** of
   incorporation. Missing this deadline attracts a daily penalty; it is the most
   common LLP mistake.
5. Then: current account, Udyam, GST if applicable.

Annual: **Form 11** (statement of partners, due 30 May) and **Form 8**
(statement of accounts, due 30 October), plus income tax return. Late fees run
per day, so diarise both.

### 3.3 Private Limited / OPC (7–15 days)

1. **DSC** for all directors and subscribers.
2. **SPICe+ Part A** — name reservation (offer 2 names, check for similarity
   against existing companies and trademarks first).
3. **SPICe+ Part B** — incorporation, plus **eMOA (INC-33)** and **eAOA
   (INC-34)**, and **AGILE-PRO-S (INC-35)** which bundles GSTIN, EPFO, ESIC,
   professional tax (in applicable states) and a bank account application.
4. Certificate of Incorporation issues with **CIN, PAN and TAN**.
5. Post-incorporation, do not miss:
   - **INC-20A** (declaration of commencement of business) within 180 days;
   - **first auditor appointed within 30 days** (Form ADT-1);
   - deposit of subscription capital into the company account.

Annual: AOC-4 (financials), MGT-7/7A (annual return), ADT-1, DIR-3 KYC per
director, board meetings and minutes, plus a **mandatory statutory audit**
regardless of turnover. Budget ₹18,000–35,000/year for compliance alone.

## 4. GST — when it applies

- **Threshold for services: ₹20 lakh** aggregate annual turnover
  (**₹10 lakh** for special-category states — the North-Eastern states,
  Uttarakhand and a few others). Verify the current list and your state's
  position with your CA.
- Below the threshold, GST registration is **optional**. Registering
  voluntarily means you must charge 18%, file returns every month/quarter, and
  your ₹5,000 session effectively becomes ₹5,900 for a walk-in family. For a
  B2C consultancy, staying unregistered while you are small is usually the
  better commercial call.
- Register voluntarily if you plan to bill **companies or schools**, who prefer
  a GST invoice for input credit.
- Registration is **mandatory regardless of turnover** in some situations,
  including inter-state supply of certain services and supply through some
  e-commerce operators — get this checked if you plan to list on a marketplace.

**Rate and classification:** consulting and coaching-type services are generally
at **18%**. The right SAC code depends on exactly what you sell (education
support vs. management consultancy vs. commercial training). Do not guess — ask
your CA to fix the SAC on your first invoice template and keep it consistent.

**Exports (counselling NRI clients abroad):** export of services is zero-rated.
File a **LUT** (Letter of Undertaking) at the start of each financial year to
export without paying IGST, and keep FIRC/bank realisation proof.

If you register: file **GSTR-1** and **GSTR-3B** on schedule (monthly, or
quarterly under QRMP), and **GSTR-9** annually where applicable. Late fees and
interest accrue automatically.

## 5. Income tax

- **Presumptive taxation** can dramatically reduce bookkeeping:
  - **Section 44ADA** (specified professions) — declare 50% of receipts as
    income. Limit ₹50 lakh, raised to **₹75 lakh** where cash receipts are
    ≤5% of turnover.
  - **Section 44AD** (business) — declare 8% of receipts (6% for digital
    receipts). Limit ₹2 crore, raised to **₹3 crore** where cash receipts are
    ≤5%.
  - **Which one applies to career counselling is a genuine grey area** — it
    turns on whether your activity is a "specified profession" under section
    44AA. Get a written opinion from your CA before you file the first return;
    switching later is messy.
- **Advance tax** in four instalments if your annual tax liability exceeds
  ₹10,000. Most consultants discover this via an interest demand.
- **TDS deducted from you:** corporate and institutional clients will deduct
  **10% under section 194J** on professional fees. Claim it in your return —
  reconcile against Form 26AS/AIS every quarter.
- **TDS deducted by you:** applies to rent above threshold, contractor payments,
  professional fees you pay out. Needs a TAN.
- Keep a **separate bank account** for the business from day one. Mixing
  personal and business money is the single biggest cause of painful audits.

## 6. Sector-specific rules that actually apply to this business

This is where a generic "how to register a company" article will fail you.

### 6.1 Advertising rules for coaching and education (important)

The CCPA's **Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisement in the
Coaching Sector, 2024** target exactly the claims this industry loves. In
substance, you must not:

- claim a **guaranteed** selection, rank, seat, admission or job;
- use a successful candidate's name, photo or testimonial **without their
  written consent obtained after the result**;
- publish success claims without disclosing the material facts behind them
  (which course the student took, whether it was paid or free, its duration);
- create false urgency ("only 2 seats left") that isn't true.

Penalties for misleading advertisements under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019
are significant (lakhs of rupees, and higher for repeat offences, plus possible
endorsement bans).

**The definition of "coaching" in those guidelines is generally understood to
exclude counselling** — but do not lean on that. This website has already been
written to comply with the substance of the rules: no guarantees anywhere, and
an explicit "we do not guarantee admission, seat, rank, visa or job" statement in
the footer of every page, on the FAQ, and in the disclaimer. **Keep it that way.**
If you ever run classes or a "test series", the separate **Guidelines for
Regulation of Coaching Centres, 2024** (registration, minimum age, facilities)
may bring you into scope — check before you launch anything class-shaped.

### 6.2 Counselling vs. clinical practice

You may call yourself a **career counsellor**, **guidance counsellor** or
**coach**. You must **not** hold yourself out as a **clinical psychologist**,
**psychiatrist** or **therapist**, or offer diagnosis or treatment, without the
relevant qualification and **RCI registration** where required. Misusing those
titles is a regulatory offence, and it is also the fastest way to a complaint.

The site is already written accordingly: the life-coaching page states plainly
that the service is non-clinical, and carries the Tele-MANAS helpline
(**14416**) for anyone who needs real mental-health support. Do not remove it.

### 6.3 Placement and recruitment

- **Domestic career consulting** (resume, interview prep, strategy) has no
  central licensing requirement. However, several states regulate **private
  placement agencies** — if you ever charge for *placing* someone in a job
  rather than advising them, check your state's rules first.
- **Overseas recruitment is strictly regulated.** Recruiting Indian workers for
  foreign employers requires a **Registration Certificate from the Protector
  General of Emigrants (MEA, eMigrate)** under the Emigration Act, 1983.
  Penalties for operating without it are severe. The study-abroad service in
  this site is written as **education guidance only**, not job placement or visa
  processing — keep that boundary sharp.
- **Never charge a candidate a fee that is contingent on getting a job.**

### 6.4 Data protection (DPDP Act, 2023) — this one is unavoidable for you

You will hold marks, scores, psychometric results and family details of
**minors**. Under the DPDP Act:

- process personal data only with **consent** for a stated purpose;
- for anyone **under 18**, obtain **verifiable parental/guardian consent**;
- **no tracking or behavioural/targeted advertising directed at children**;
- publish a notice (your privacy policy) explaining what you collect and why;
- appoint a contact point for grievances and respond within the prescribed time;
- delete data when the purpose is served.

Penalties under the Act are large (up to ₹250 crore for the most serious
breaches — that scale is aimed at big platforms, but the obligations apply to
everyone). Compliance rules were notified with phased timelines — **confirm the
current position with your advisor.**

The website already does its part: the enquiry form has an explicit consent
checkbox, a "student is under 18, submitted by a parent/guardian" checkbox, a
privacy policy naming a grievance officer, no ad trackers, and **self-hosted
fonts** so no third party receives your visitors' IP addresses.

### 6.5 Communication rules

- Bulk SMS requires **DLT registration** (TRAI) with a registered sender ID and
  approved templates. Plan a week for it.
- Marketing to numbers on the **DND registry** without consent invites
  complaints and penalties.
- **WhatsApp**: use the WhatsApp Business app for one-to-one replies. Bulk
  broadcasting through unofficial tools gets numbers banned — if you need
  templated messaging at scale, use the official WhatsApp Business API through a
  provider.

## 7. Trademark (optional but worth it once you have traction)

- Relevant classes: **Class 41** (education, training, coaching) and
  **Class 35** (business management, advertising, employment/recruitment
  services). Most consultancies file 41; add 35 if you do job-side work.
- Government fee: **₹4,500 per class** for individuals/startups/small
  enterprises (Udyam certificate is what proves "small enterprise"), **₹9,000**
  otherwise. Agent fees ₹3,000–8,000.
- **Do a free public search first** on the IP India trademark search portal, and
  check the domain and social handles in the same sitting.
- Once filed you can use **™** immediately; **®** only after registration.
- Registration typically takes 12–24 months if unopposed.

---

## 8. Insurance and contracts worth having

- **Professional indemnity insurance** — covers claims that your advice caused
  loss. Small covers start around ₹5,000–15,000/year. Genuinely worth it in an
  advisory business.
- **A one-page engagement letter** for every paid client: scope, fee, what is
  not included, no-guarantee clause, confidentiality, refund terms. The
  [terms of service](../src/pages/legal/terms.html) page is the online version;
  a signed one-pager is stronger for larger packages.
- **Consent form** for minors, signed by the parent, before any assessment.
- **Testimonial consent form** — required in substance by the advertising rules
  above, and simple decency.

## 9. Recurring compliance calendar (proprietorship + GST)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 11th & 20th monthly (or quarterly under QRMP) | GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B |
| 15 Jun / 15 Sep / 15 Dec / 15 Mar | Advance tax instalments |
| 31 July | Income tax return (non-audit cases) |
| 31 Oct | Income tax return (audit cases) |
| 31 Dec | GSTR-9 annual return, where applicable |
| Anytime | Update Udyam details if turnover/investment changes |

For LLPs add Form 11 (30 May) and Form 8 (30 Oct). For companies add AOC-4,
MGT-7, ADT-1 and DIR-3 KYC. **Put every one of these in a calendar with a
two-week warning.** Late fees in India are per-day and unforgiving.

## 10. The 10-minute version

1. Register on **Udyam** today. Free, instant, gets you taken seriously.
2. Trade as a **sole proprietorship** until there is a reason not to.
3. Open a **current account** and never mix personal money with it.
4. **Skip GST** until you approach ₹20 lakh — unless you are billing companies.
5. Get a CA on a small retainer (₹1,000–2,000/month) from month one.
6. **Never promise a rank, seat, admission, visa or job** — in an ad, on a call,
   or on WhatsApp.
7. Get **written parental consent** for every client under 18.
8. Do not use protected titles like "psychologist" unless you hold the
   qualification and registration.
9. File the **trademark** once the name is proven, not before.
10. Buy **professional indemnity insurance** before your first ₹50,000 package.
