# 06 · SEO, marketing and getting the first clients

A website with no traffic is a business card in a drawer. This is the plan for
the first 90 days, in priority order.

---

## 1. Do these five things in week one (free, highest return)

### 1.1 Google Business Profile — the single highest-ROI listing

For a local consultancy this beats everything else combined. "Career counsellor
near me" searches land here, not on your homepage.

1. Create it at business.google.com with the exact business name, address and phone.
2. Category: **Career Guidance Service** (add "Educational Consultant",
   "Counselor" as secondary).
3. Verify (postcard, phone or video — video is fastest for service businesses).
4. Fill **everything**: hours, services with prices, description, website link,
   WhatsApp link, 10+ photos.
5. **Ask every satisfied client for a review.** Send the direct review link over
   WhatsApp the day after the session. Reply to every review, good or bad.

Twenty genuine reviews will out-perform ₹50,000 of ads for local searches.

### 1.2 Google Search Console
Add the property, verify by DNS, submit `https://yourbrand.com/sitemap.xml`.
Check "Pages" weekly for indexing problems, and "Queries" monthly to see what
people actually searched before landing on you.

### 1.3 Google Analytics (optional)
Create a GA4 property, paste the `G-XXXXXXX` ID into `site.config.json` →
`analytics.ga4Id`, rebuild. Leave it empty if you'd rather not run any tracking
— the site works identically and your privacy story stays simpler.

### 1.4 WhatsApp Business
Install the **WhatsApp Business** app (not regular WhatsApp). Set the business
name, category, hours, a greeting message, an away message, and a catalogue with
your three packages. Most of your enquiries will arrive here — the site's
floating button and the form's fallback both point at it.

### 1.5 Bing Webmaster Tools
Two minutes, imports from Search Console, and Bing still sends a slice of
traffic (and increasingly feeds AI assistants).

## 2. What people actually search

Build content around these clusters. The intent, not the volume, is what matters.

**High-intent, high-value (write these first)**
- `career counsellor in <your city>`
- `neet counselling help <year>`
- `neet <score> kaunsa college milega`
- `jee josaa choice filling help`
- `class 10 ke baad kya kare`
- `career counselling fees in india`
- `psychometric test for students <city>`

**Informational (builds authority and email/WhatsApp lists)**
- `neet ke baad options agar mbbs na mile`
- `branch vs college jee`
- `career change after 30 india`
- `mba vs ms after engineering`
- `study abroad worth it or not india`

**Local (fastest to rank)**
- `<city> career counsellor for neet students`
- `best career guidance near me`

The three guides already on the site target the second cluster. **Write the
first cluster next** — those pages convert.

## 3. A 12-month content calendar

One post a month is enough if each is genuinely useful. Publish alongside the
season it serves.

| Month | Topic | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| Jan | "Class 10 ke baad stream kaise chune — a decision framework" | Board exam season |
| Feb | "Class 12 done: every option beyond engineering and medical" | Pre-result anxiety |
| Mar | "Psychometric tests explained: what they can and can't tell you" | Assessment season |
| Apr | "JEE Main result out — what to do in the next 7 days" | Result timing |
| May | "NEET is over. Now what?" | Result timing |
| Jun | "JoSAA choice filling: a step-by-step walkthrough" | Peak counselling |
| Jul | "NEET counselling round by round" *(already written)* | Peak counselling |
| Aug | "Dropper year: an honest cost-benefit analysis" | Post-admission regret window |
| Sep | "Study abroad: the real 5-year cost model" | Intake planning |
| Oct | "Campus placement prep for final-year students" | Placement season |
| Nov | "Career switch after 30" *(already written)* | Appraisal season thinking |
| Dec | "Choosing between two job offers" | Notice-period season |

**Format that works:** 1,200–2,000 words, one clear framework, one table, no
fluff, and an honest "here's when you *don't* need us" section. Every post ends
with the free-call CTA (already the pattern in the three existing guides).

## 4. Social media, realistically

You cannot do four platforms well. Pick two.

| Platform | Audience | Post what | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | Students + parents | Reels answering one question in 45 s; carousel "5 options after NEET"; result-season Q&A | High but highest reach |
| **YouTube** | Parents researching seriously | 8–15 min explainers; these rank in Google and convert months later | High effort, long shelf life |
| **LinkedIn** | Working professionals | Career-switch posts, hiring-market observations | Low effort, feeds the B2C professional line |
| **Facebook** | Parents in tier-2/3 cities | Local groups, community answers | Underrated for this audience |

**Recommendation:** Instagram + YouTube for the student side; LinkedIn for the
professional side. One Reel a week beats a burst of thirty and then silence.

**Content rule:** answer a real question completely and for free. The audience
that trusts you enough to book has usually consumed 5–10 free pieces first.

## 5. Offline and partnership channels (often the fastest ₹)

1. **Schools** — offer a free 45-minute stream-selection session for Class 9/10.
   Schools want this and rarely have it. It also puts you in front of 200 parents.
2. **Coaching institutes** — they teach; they do not counsel. A referral
   arrangement can work, but **never accept a per-admission commission** — that
   is precisely the conflict this business is positioned against.
3. **Colleges** — placement cells need interview-prep workshops.
4. **Corporates** — outplacement and career-development workshops pay well and
   are B2B (steady, invoiced, GST-friendly).
5. **Parent WhatsApp groups** — answer questions helpfully, never spam. One
   useful answer generates more calls than ten forwards.
6. **Local press / community radio** — a monthly column on career decisions in a
   regional paper is cheap authority.

## 6. Paid ads — only after organic works

Do not start here. Ads amplify a working funnel; they cannot create one.

When you do:
- **Google Search ads** on high-intent terms (`career counsellor <city>`,
  `neet counselling guidance`). Expect ₹15–60 per click in this niche,
  seasonally higher.
- Start at **₹300–500/day**, one campaign, exact-match keywords only.
- Send traffic to the **specific service page**, never the homepage.
- Track cost per enquiry, then cost per booked session. If a booked client is
  worth ₹5,000 and costs ₹1,200 to acquire, scale. If not, stop.
- **Meta ads** work for awareness in the student segment but convert worse for
  high-ticket counselling. Test with ₹200/day before believing anyone's promises.

## 7. Email and WhatsApp follow-up

- Collect email on the enquiry form (already there, optional field).
- A simple 4-email sequence after a discovery call — recap, a relevant guide,
  a case study, a "still deciding?" note — recovers a meaningful share of
  fence-sitters.
- **Respect DND and DLT rules** for SMS ([01](01-legal-company-registration.md) §6.5).
- Seasonal broadcast (NEET result week, JoSAA opening) to past enquirers who
  consented is the highest-converting message you will ever send.

---

## 8. Measure only these five numbers

| Metric | Where | Healthy signal |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiries per week | Form + WhatsApp count | Growing month on month |
| Enquiry → discovery call | Your own tally | > 60% |
| Discovery call → paid client | Your own tally | 20–40% |
| Cost per enquiry (if running ads) | Ads dashboard | < 10% of average client value |
| Organic sessions | Search Console | Rising over 3-month windows |

Ignore follower counts. They do not pay for anything.

## 9. The first ten clients

Realistically, they come from: **family and friends' networks (3–4)**, **one
school workshop (2–3)**, **Google Business Profile local searches (1–2)**, and
**social media (1–2)**. The website's job at this stage is not discovery — it is
**conversion**: someone hears your name, searches it, and decides in ninety
seconds whether you look like a real professional or a fly-by-night operator.

That is exactly what this site was built to win.
